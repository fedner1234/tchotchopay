Why Remittances Matter in Haiti

Remittances are the backbone of Haiti's economy. In 2024, Haitian families received $4.1 billion from the diaspora — nearly 20% of the country's GDP. About 70% of that comes from the United States, with large communities in Florida, New York, Massachusetts, and Canada.

For millions of families in Port-au-Prince, Cap-Haïtien, Les Cayes, and across the countryside, money sent from abroad pays for food, school fees, rent, electricity, and medical care. When fees are high or transfers are slow, real people suffer.

The average cost of sending $200 to Haiti in 2025 was around 5.5% — more than double the United Nations' target of 3%. On a $200 transfer, that's $11 taken from your family before they see a single goud. Over a year of monthly transfers, that's over $130 lost in fees alone.

💡 The bottom line

Choosing the right service can save you $50–$200 per year on remittances. The differences between providers are significant — both in fees and in how fast money arrives.

Delivery Options: How Recipients Can Receive Money

When you send money to Haiti, your recipient can receive it in several ways. Understanding the options helps you choose what's best for your family's situation.

📱 MonCash (Most Popular)

MonCash is Haiti's most widely used mobile wallet, operated by Digicel. With over 2 million registered users, it lets recipients receive money directly on their phone — no bank account required. Recipients can use their MonCash balance to pay bills, buy goods at merchants, send money to others, or withdraw cash at any Digicel agent.

To receive money on MonCash, your recipient just needs an active Digicel phone number. Transfers typically arrive in under 5 minutes.

📱 NatCash

NatCash is the mobile wallet from Natcom (National Telecom S.A.), Haiti's second largest telecom. It works similarly to MonCash and is especially popular in areas with strong Natcom coverage. NatCash only works with Natcom numbers and handles transfers in Haitian Gourdes (HTG).

🏦 Bank Account Transfer

Recipients with accounts at Haitian banks (BNC, Unibank, Sogebank, BPH) can receive transfers directly. Bank transfers are more formal and sometimes required for larger amounts, but they take 1–3 business days and require the recipient to have an active bank account — which many Haitians don't have.

💵 Cash Pickup

Traditional services like Western Union and MoneyGram offer cash pickup at agents around Haiti. The recipient shows ID and picks up cash. This works even without a phone or bank account, but fees are typically highest for this option.

⚠️ Watch out for exchange rate markups

Some providers advertise "no fee" transfers but make money on the exchange rate. Always check how many gourdes your recipient will actually receive, not just the fee percentage. A 0% fee with a 5% exchange rate markup costs more than a 2% fee at the mid-market rate.

Exchange Rates & The Hidden Cost

The USD to Haitian Gourde (HTG) exchange rate has been volatile in recent years. As of late 2026, 1 USD buys approximately 130–140 HTG on the open market — but many remittance services offer 10–15% below this rate, quietly taking a cut on every transfer.

Here's a real example: If the mid-market rate is 135 HTG per dollar and you send $200, your recipient should get 27,000 HTG. But if the service uses a rate of 120 HTG per dollar, they only get 24,000 HTG — that's 3,000 gourdes (about $22) quietly taken from your family.

Sending to MonCash: Complete Guide

MonCash is the easiest and fastest way for your family to receive money in Haiti. Here's everything you need to know.

What your recipient needs

  • An active Digicel Haiti phone number
  • A registered MonCash account (free to set up via *202# or the MonCash app)
  • Their account must be active and verified — fully verified accounts have higher receive limits

MonCash receive limits

MonCash has daily and monthly receive limits set by Digicel:

  • Minimum transfer: 785 HTG (~$6)
  • Maximum per transaction: 100,000 HTG (~$740)
  • Monthly limit varies by KYC level (basic vs. full KYC)

What recipients can do with their MonCash balance

  • Pay bills (electricity, water, internet)
  • Buy goods at MonCash merchants (supermarkets, pharmacies, gas stations)
  • Send money to other MonCash users for free
  • Withdraw cash at any Digicel agent or MonCash ATM
  • Buy airtime and data
✅ Pro tip

Encourage your recipient to keep their MonCash balance in gourdes for everyday spending. This avoids withdrawal fees and is more convenient than cash for bill payments and merchant purchases.

Sending to NatCash: Complete Guide

NatCash is operated by Natcom and is particularly strong in northern Haiti. If your family uses Natcom as their carrier, NatCash may be more convenient than MonCash.

Key differences from MonCash

  • Only works with Natcom phone numbers (not Digicel)
  • Transfers must be in whole gourdes — no decimals accepted
  • Strong coverage in Cap-Haïtien, Gonaïves, and northern departments
  • Integrates with CamTransfer and Unitransfer for international remittances

What your recipient needs

  • An active Natcom phone number
  • A registered NatCash account

The USDC Option: Hold Digital Dollars in Haiti

The Haitian gourde (HTG) has lost significant value over the past decade. In 2015, $1 USD bought roughly 50 HTG. Today it buys over 130 HTG — meaning gourdes have lost over 60% of their value against the dollar in 10 years. Families who hold gourdes watch their savings erode.
